The Dice: Build Guide

A 3 inch steel die from 14 gauge sheet. You cut two flat blanks, bend each one into a U on the brake, fit the two U's into a cube, MIG weld eight seams, then grind and finish it. Opposite faces add to 7.

One chance

Your first blank pair is on the shop. A second pair costs $5.00, the price of the material. That is why every step below starts on scrap. Nothing gets cut, bent or welded on your real blank until you have done that step on scrap first.

Earn the weld

No portfolio, no dice welding. The die costs more steel and is harder to weld than any coupon. Your four joint portfolio is your evidence you earned it.

Before you weld your die, your portfolio display is on the shelf, self graded, clean, and checked by Mr. McAteer. Not there yet? Weld more coupons first. Bending and fitting can go on while you do.

How it is graded

Everyone gets the same four rows, 25 points each, 100 total, on the dice self grade sheet. A is full credit, B is 80 percent, C is 70 percent. Try again means fix it and bring it back.

  1. Square: 3 inches on every side within 1/16 inch, every corner square, faces flat.
  2. Weld quality: all eight seams fused full length, even bead, no burn through, no undercut.
  3. Bend quality: four clean 90 degree bends on the lines, no cracks, bend record filled in. Opposite faces add to 7.
  4. Finishing quality: welds blended flush, pips clean, no gouges, an even finish done on purpose.

Turn in: no photos. Grade every row yourself, then set your die on the sheet, with your bend record, on the shelf under the Learn to Weld poster.

Late: one day late loses 30 percent, after that 50 percent. Exceptions are Mr. McAteer's call, for students who use class time, work safely and wear the right gear.

Brake 101

  1. Practice first. Bend two scrap strips of the same gauge and check them with a square before you touch your blank.
  2. Line the bend line up with the nose of the clamping finger. Set the finger parallel to the bending leaf.
  3. Clamp tight. Hands out of the clamp and out of the leaf's swing. One person on the handles.
  4. Bend a little past 90. Steel springs back. Check it with a square, then write it on your bend record.

Set up the welder

Be very careful here. Wrong settings blow through 14 gauge.

  1. Gas is 75/25. The gas switch is on the back of the welder. Set right, its handle is pulled straight toward the welder. If it is not, stop and ask before you weld.
  2. Set wire speed and voltage from the chart inside the welder's side door for your gauge.
  3. When in doubt, start colder. Use the chart settings for 16, 18 or even 20 gauge and run a bead on scrap first.
  4. Tack every corner, check it with a square, then weld a half inch at a time and skip around so the heat does not build in one spot.

Blow through is the most common way to lose a die. Heat builds at a thin edge until the puddle falls out and leaves a hole.

  1. Short trigger pulls. How long you hold the trigger matters more than anything else.
  2. Blew a hole? Stop. Turn the welder down one step, let it cool, then fill the hole from its edges in short bursts.
  3. Clean metal, a tight fit and tacks on every corner. A gap is where blow through starts.

Clean up the welds and finish

Tools: the angle grinder with a flap disc, the DA sander, and the pneumatic die grinder with its abrasive pads. The finish is your call, brushed, polished or patterned. Do it on purpose.

  1. Let the cube cool before you grind.
  2. Flap disc first, held nearly flat, about 30 degrees. Blend each weld flush without cutting into the face next to it.
  3. Look hard at every seam after that first pass. A pinhole, a low spot or a gap needs more filler wire. Grinding cannot fill a hole. Weld it with short trigger pulls, let it cool, then flap disc it flush again.
  4. Die grinder pads in the corners and around the pips, then the DA sander on the faces. Step up in grit as you go.
  5. Face shield over your glasses, guard on the grinder, gloves on, both hands on the tool. Thin steel heats fast, so keep moving.
