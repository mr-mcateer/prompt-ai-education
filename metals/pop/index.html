Plan of Procedure (POP)

Your own build starts here. A POP is your plan on paper: every step, every tool, every part. Mr. McAteer initials each step before you move to the next one.

Print the POP form (PDF)

How much detail

  1. Every step, in order. Write every step of the build. You do not explain how to run a machine you are certified on. You do name any fixture, jig, stop block or magnet setup you use, and how you set it.
  2. Every step has its numbers. Not "cut the steel." Write "Cut 2 pieces of 1 x 1 x 1/8 angle to 6 1/4 in on the horizontal band saw." Not "weld it." Write "MIG tack all four corners, check square, then weld 1 in stitches, skipping around."
  3. Someone else could build it. There are many ways to cut, bend and join steel in this shop. A classmate should be able to read your step and do it your way without asking you.
  4. Every tool needs your cert. Write the tool on every step. If you do not hold that cert yet, get it before you reach that step. No cert, no step.
  5. Weld steps name the process (MIG, stick or TIG), the joint, the metal thickness, and "settings from the chart."
  6. CNC plasma steps name the DXF file and the gauge, and follow the Plasma route, steps 0 to 6. Teacher OK before every cut.

Bill of materials (BOM)

  • List everything you need. Skip welding wire, gas, grinding discs and paint. List every bolt, nut, washer, hinge and screw.
  • Sizes are finished sizes. Use inches. Sheet goes by gauge, bar and tube by thickness.

Total steel needed

  • Add up your BOM by shape and size. Example: flat bar 3/16 x 1 1/2, 14 in total. Sheet 10 ga, one 4 x 4 in part.
  • Keep each shape and size separate. Flat bar and angle never share a total.
  • Add waste for every saw cut and for squaring the ends. Ask Mr. McAteer how much for your saw.
  • Plasma parts: price them on the Plasma Shop Calculator before you cut. Show your math here.

Three-view drawing

  • Three views on one page: front view lower left, top view directly above it, right side view to the right of the front view.
  • Straight edge, to scale, and every feature dimensioned. Hole sizes and hole centers too.
  • SolidWorks or Onshape is fine and often faster. It is not required.
  • Plasma parts: write the DXF file name next to the view that shows that part.
  • Draw this first. A drawing is where you make your decisions.

Turn it in

Agree on a point value with Mr. McAteer and write it at the top. Keep the POP with your project. It comes back to him with every step initialed.
